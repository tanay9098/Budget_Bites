# Induction cooking and cookware

Source: Hawkins Cookers, additional information leaflet for the induction compatible Futura Hard Anodised Cook-n-Serve Bowl ([https://www.hawkinscookers.com/Cookbooks/2%20Litre,%203%20Litre%20%26%204%20Litre%20Induction%20Compatible.pdf](https://www.hawkinscookers.com/Cookbooks/2%20Litre,%203%20Litre%20%26%204%20Litre%20Induction%20Compatible.pdf)), and the Hawkins Hevibase manual ([https://www.hawkinscookers.com/Cookbooks/Hawkins%20Hevibase%20IM.pdf](https://www.hawkinscookers.com/Cookbooks/Hawkins%20Hevibase%20IM.pdf)). These are manufacturer notes for specific products, not general rules.

- Hawkins says the induction compatible bowl has a plate of AISI 430 grade magnetic stainless steel permanently attached to the base, which is what makes it induction compatible.
- Hawkins says induction cooktops initially heat up faster than gas and electric stoves, so the times normally needed for initial heating of oil, butter or ghee may need to be reduced.
- Hawkins says to ensure the hot bowl is never without food, because dry heating may permanently damage it.
- The Hevibase pressure cooker manual says the Hevibase is not suitable for induction stoves. Check your own cooker's manual before using a pressure cooker on an induction cooktop.
