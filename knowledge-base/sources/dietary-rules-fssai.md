# Vegetarian and non-vegetarian food - FSSAI definitions

Source: Food Safety and Standards (Packaging and Labelling) Regulations, 2011 - compendium (version of 04 Aug 2021) ([https://www.fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Compendium_Packaging_Labelling_Regulations_04_08_2021.pdf](https://www.fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Compendium_Packaging_Labelling_Regulations_04_08_2021.pdf)), regulation 1.2.1. These are the legal definitions used to label packaged food in India. BudgetBites applies the same classification to home recipes.

> "Non-Vegetarian Food" means an article of food which contains whole or part of any animal including birds, fresh water or marine animals or eggs or products of any animal origin, but excluding milk or milk products, as an ingredient.

> "Vegetarian Food" means any article of Food other than Non-Vegetarian Food.

What this means in BudgetBites:
- Eggs, chicken and other meat, fish and seafood make a dish non-vegetarian.
- Milk and milk products (curd, ghee, paneer, butter) do not make a dish non-vegetarian under this definition; BudgetBites still lets users exclude dairy.
- The regulation separately allows a label to declare when egg is the only non-vegetarian ingredient.
- Packaged masalas, stock cubes and other prepared ingredients must be checked on their labels for animal-derived ingredients.
