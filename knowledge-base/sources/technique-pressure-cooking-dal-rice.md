# Cooking dal and rice in a pressure cooker

Source: Hawkins Hevibase instruction manual and recipe book (Issue 2, Sep 2015) ([https://www.hawkinscookers.com/Cookbooks/Hawkins%20Hevibase%20IM.pdf](https://www.hawkinscookers.com/Cookbooks/Hawkins%20Hevibase%20IM.pdf)), "Cooking Dal" p. 20, and the Hawkins All Indian Cookbook ([https://www.hawkinscookers.com/Cookbooks/AllIndianCookbook.pdf](https://www.hawkinscookers.com/Cookbooks/AllIndianCookbook.pdf)), "Cooking Rice" p. 42. Paraphrased with short quotations.

## Dal
- Dal is the Indian term for various dried edible seeds such as lentils.
- Some dals such as tuvar, moong and chana tend to sprout (froth out of the vent) while cooking.
- To reduce sprouting, do not fill the cooker body (dal and water combined) to more than one-third, soak the dal for 15 minutes before cooking, reduce the heat to medium or lower as soon as the cooker reaches full operating pressure, and do not release pressure by lifting the vent weight.
- The manual's example: 3/4 cup (150 g) of tuvar dal with 2 cups (480 ml) water can be cooked in a 1 litre mold standing on the grid, with 1 cup (240 ml) of water in the cooker, for 10 minutes.
- The manual says there are many varieties of dal and that some experimentation with water and cooking time may be needed to suit your taste.

## Rice
- The basic steps are: put water and rice in the cooker, close it, bring to full pressure on high heat, reduce the heat and cook for the required time, then release pressure as the chart directs.
- Do not fill the cooker more than half when cooking rice.
- Wash rice by rinsing in water until the water is clear.
- One cup of dry rice yields 2 to 3 cups of cooked rice.
- Some varieties of rice, especially white rice, froth out of the vent tube unless they are cooked with oil or butter or in a mold.
